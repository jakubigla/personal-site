# Jakub Igla - personal site (jacops.pl) - product brief (MVP)

Status: draft v4, 2026-11-27 (v3 decisions kept; visual direction and motion expanded from two
references, see "Inspiration"). Input for the first xpm run (`/xpm:xpm-autopilot brief.md`).
Style rule for all copy and code comments: plain "-" only, never an em or en dash.

## Problem

jacops.pl today is three icons (GitHub, LinkedIn, a CV PDF from 2020) under the title
"JacOps - Cloud Native and Cyber Security". It says nothing about what Jakub does now:
data platform architecture on Databricks, pre-sales, and leading platform teams. His LinkedIn
"About" is also from the DevOps era. Anyone who checks him after a LinkedIn post, an RFP or a
referral finds no page that proves the current profile or says how to engage him.

## What we want

One page that works as a living CV and as a soft offer for B2B engagements through his
one-person company. The brand is the person: "Jakub Igla". JacOps appears only as the legal
entity in the footer. The page must look like it was built by someone fluent in AI-assisted
engineering: creative, purposeful animation, and an explicit "how this site was built" note.
It launches with a LinkedIn post, so the link preview and the first screen matter most.

## Users

Priority order:
1. Consultancy partner - a firm that subcontracts architects and pre-sales support. Wants proof
   of delivery at scale, RFP experience and the ability to lead a team.
2. End client - Head of Data, CTO or platform lead who needs an architect, an audit or a
   target architecture. Wants to see similar problems solved and measurable outcomes.
3. Databricks ecosystem - field teams and partners looking for a Champion or a Resident
   Solutions Architect. Wants the badge, certifications and governance depth.
4. Jakub (owner) - updates content a few times a year by editing one file and merging a PR.

Recruiters are not a target, but the page must still read as a CV in 60 seconds.

## Visual direction

- Tone: confident, technical, modern. It should be obvious that AI tooling was used well, and
  equally obvious that an architect made the decisions. Enterprise buyers must not read it as a toy.
- Ambition: motion is the portfolio piece. The bar is "people forward the link because of how it
  moves", while every fact stays readable in plain HTML. Motion always tells the story of the
  content (governance, scale, delivery); it is never decoration for its own sake.

### Inspiration (techniques only - no assets, copy or layout copied)

1. "AI Engineer Portfolio Website" (Figma Community, Figma Make, CC BY 4.0). Take:
   - Deep navy, near-black canvas with a living constellation of nodes and thin glowing edges
     behind every section (a "plexus" network).
   - Name set large in a gradient (violet to magenta to electric blue); section titles each get
     their own gradient accent.
   - Glass cards: translucent surface, hairline border, soft inner glow on hover, small icon tile
     top left, tech tags as rounded pills.
   - Vertical timeline on a centre line with a gradient stroke, cards alternating sides, a node
     dot on the line per role.
   - Gentle fade-and-rise reveal per section as it enters the viewport.
   Leave behind: the contact form, phone number, skill percentage bars, emoji avatars and
   generic "AI/ML" copy. A principal architect does not rate himself "Terraform 90%".
2. "The Last Boring Slide" (a Claude artifact, a scroll-and-click narrative deck). Take:
   - Subversion as the hook: it opens as a deliberately dull, default-styled slide, then a
     visible editing cursor selects the title and rewrites it ("This slide is bad.") before the
     real design takes over.
   - The camera pulls back: the flat slide turns out to be a physical sheet floating in a large
     cinematic space, with a reflection under it.
   - Data that outgrows its frame: a chart line goes exponential, runs off the top of its sheet,
     new sheets stack on top into a tower, and the camera rides the line upward.
   - Big, calm editorial statements set beside the moving object, one idea per step.
   - Chapters with their own URL hash, so a step can be linked and returns in the same state.

### Signature sequence (one continuous story, driven by scroll)

To be validated at the mockup gate; the acts can be cut or merged, the story should not be lost.

- Act 1 - "The last boring homepage" (first load, about 4 s, plays once, skippable by any
  scroll, key or tap). The page opens as a faithful recreation of today's jacops.pl: three icons
  and "JacOps - Cloud Native and Cyber Security" in default styling. An editor cursor appears,
  selects the title, deletes it and types the new headline. The flat page then tilts back in 3D,
  shrinks into one sheet in a dark navy space and the real hero assembles around it. On repeat
  visits in the same session, skip straight to the final state. This is the one place the old
  "JacOps" title may appear outside the footer, and only for the second before it is rewritten.
- Act 2 - "Chaos to governed". As the visitor scrolls past the hero, the loose, drifting
  constellation snaps into order: nodes sort into layers (people and groups, catalogs,
  datasets), and access paths draw from identity to data one by one, each lighting up as it is
  granted. The background network becomes the platform being built, not random particles.
  The practice areas are introduced as labels on this graph. All names on it are invented.
- Act 3 - "It left the card". In the proof strip, one scale figure (for example datasets
  governed or streams run) is drawn as a small line or bar that keeps growing past the edge of
  its card; the card stacks a second and third panel to hold it, then settles. Other numbers
  count up once.
- Act 4 - "The paved road". Selected work cards slide in along the graph edges as glass
  cards; hovering or focusing a card highlights the part of the graph it relates to.
- Act 5 - "The delivery line". The timeline's centre line draws with scroll progress, each role
  node lights as it passes, the gradient shifts from the early-career colour to the current one.
- Act 6 - "Settle". At contact, the constellation slows to stillness and the camera comes to
  rest on the three contact links. The page ends calm, not busy.

### Motion system

- One scroll timeline drives the whole page (CSS scroll-driven animations where supported,
  a small IntersectionObserver plus requestAnimationFrame fallback otherwise). No scroll
  hijacking, no fixed scroll lengths that trap the user, native scroll speed always.
- 3D is CSS transforms with perspective plus Canvas 2D or SVG for the graph. WebGL is allowed
  only if a prototype proves it fits the performance budget, and then only as a lazy-loaded
  enhancement after the page is interactive.
- Easing: one shared set of curves and durations as design tokens; nothing faster than 150 ms
  or slower than 900 ms except the Act 1 sequence.
- Pointer: subtle parallax of the constellation (a few pixels) on desktop; none on touch.
- Chapters: each act maps to a section id, so `#work` or `#timeline` deep-links into the final
  state of that act.

### Motion budget and fallbacks

- Nothing blocks reading. The hero text is real HTML, painted in the first frame and is the
  LCP element; Act 1 is drawn on top of it and never delays it.
- Nothing loops forever: the constellation drifts only while the visitor scrolls or moves the
  pointer, then eases to rest within about 2 s; all animation pauses when the tab is hidden or
  the element is off screen.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: Act 1 is skipped, the graph is shown fully drawn and static, counts
  show final values, reveals become simple opacity changes or none.
- JavaScript off: the page reads top to bottom as a styled CV with the final state of every act.
- Low-power devices (hardware concurrency or device memory low, or frame time over budget for a
  second): drop to the reduced variant automatically.
- Colour contrast stays WCAG 2.2 AA over the animated background in both themes (the light
  theme uses the same graph in ink on paper rather than glow on navy).

### Design gate

Mockups are approved by Jakub before build (xpm design gate): hero, one work card, mobile hero,
plus a motion prototype of Act 1 and Act 2 (a short screen recording or a throwaway HTML file is
enough) with a reading of its JavaScript size and frame rate on a mid-range phone.

## Constraints

- Static site only. No server, no database, no form backend.
- Hosting on AWS in Jakub's existing account, where the jacops.pl zone already lives in Route 53:
  private S3 bucket, CloudFront with origin access control, ACM certificate, HTTPS only with
  HTTP redirected, security headers set at CloudFront.
- Cost target: under 2 USD per month at expected traffic. No always-on compute, no NAT, no WAF plan.
- Infrastructure in Terraform. An older module of his exists
  (`terraform-website-s3-cloudfront-route53`, about 2021) and may be reused or replaced.
- Deploy from GitHub Actions with OIDC to AWS. No long-lived keys anywhere.
- xpm autopilot does not deploy: the first `terraform apply` and the DNS cut-over from the
  current page are an attended step by Jakub. Everything up to that is automated and tested.
- CI must run on every pull request, docs-only ones included (xpm autopilot requirement).
- Performance on a mid-range phone: Lighthouse 95+ for performance, accessibility, best
  practices and SEO; LCP under 2.5 s; total JavaScript kept small enough to meet that.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA.
- No cookies and no third-party trackers, so no consent banner is needed.
- No third-party fonts or scripts loaded from external origins at runtime.
- Language: English only at launch (decided). Structure must allow a Polish version later.
- The repository is public from the first commit: no secrets, no client names, no private
  contact details beyond what the page itself shows.
- Framework choice is open (decided in the architecture stage), but the output must be static
  files and the content file must stay the single source of truth.
