## Capability
Jakub sees how many visits the site gets, through a cookieless counter (CloudFront logs or a privacy-first service).

## Acceptance
- AC1: Jakub can see a visit count per day.
- AC2: The counter sets no cookie and needs no consent banner.
- AC3: The counter loads no script from an external origin at runtime.
- AC4: Visit counts come only from CloudFront access logs; no hosted analytics or privacy-first third-party service is used.
- AC5: Visitor IP addresses are truncated before counts are stored (IPv4 last octet and IPv6 last 80 bits zeroed), and no stored count or derived file holds a full IP address.
- AC6: Raw log objects expire through an S3 lifecycle rule, defined in Terraform, after no more than 30 days.
- AC7: The change that turns CloudFront logging on also updates the footer privacy note (FR-021) to name the logs, the IP minimisation and the retention period. Logging stays off until that change ships.

## Notes
Analytics that set cookies are out of scope; no third-party scripts from external origins at runtime.

Elicitation 2026-11-27 Q9: stays Could, after launch, CloudFront logs only. The "privacy-first service" option in the Capability is dropped because it would be a third-party origin (NFR-017).

Logging is off at launch (NFR-031). This FR is the only path that turns it on, and only under AC5 to AC7. The 30-day ceiling comes from the research's GDPR access-log finding; the PO confirms the exact period when the ticket is shaped.

Log storage and counting must stay inside the 2 USD per month cap (NFR-019).
