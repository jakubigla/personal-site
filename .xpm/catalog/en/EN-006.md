## Outcome
Build the one scroll timeline engine that drives every act: CSS scroll-driven animations where supported and a small IntersectionObserver plus requestAnimationFrame fallback elsewhere, with native scroll speed and no scroll hijacking. It maps acts to section ids so a hash deep-links into an act's final state, pauses work when the tab is hidden or an element is off screen, and drops automatically to the reduced variant on prefers-reduced-motion, with JavaScript off, or on low-power devices, so content never depends on animation.

## Acceptance
- AC1: A demo section on the skeleton page animates with scroll progress using the engine, in a browser with and without native scroll-driven animation support.
- AC2: With prefers-reduced-motion set, the engine shows final states with opacity-only or no transitions.
- AC3: With JavaScript disabled, every section renders in its final state and all text is visible.
- AC4: When low hardware concurrency or device memory is reported, or frame time exceeds budget for one second, the engine switches to the reduced variant.
- AC5: Loading the page with a section hash such as #work scrolls to that section with its act in the final state.
- AC6: Animation callbacks stop while the tab is hidden, and the engine never prevents default scrolling.

## Notes
Low-power thresholds and frame budget are named constants: 4 or fewer cores or 2 GB or less memory, or a median frame over 25 ms for 1 s (ADR-0006). The Act 1 gate is a same-origin head script under 1 KB (ADR-0004). WebGL is allowed only if a prototype proves it fits the performance budget, and then only as a lazy-loaded enhancement after the page is interactive.
